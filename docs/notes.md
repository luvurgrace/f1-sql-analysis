## Q1 Findings

Michael Schumacher in the 2000s (45.9% win rate) and Max Verstappen in the 2020s (44.4%, decade still incomplete) achieved the highest decade-level win rates in the dataset.

The 1980s appear to be one of the most competitive eras. Alain Prost led the decade with a win rate of only 25.2%, the lowest among all decade leaders, suggesting that victories were shared among several elite drivers.

Lewis Hamilton is the only driver to appear in the top three across three different decades. However, win rate tends to favor shorter but highly successful careers or periods, as seen with Ayrton Senna in the 1990s and Kimi Antonelli in the 2020s.

## Q2 Findings

Ferrari in the 2000s (48.9%), Red Bull in the 2020s (47.9%, decade incomplete), and Mercedes in the 2010s (47.0%) represent the most dominant constructor-decades in the dataset. In each case, the leading team won nearly half of all races it entered during the decade.

The 1970s were the most competitive era for constructors. Team Lotus (26.3%) and Ferrari (26.2%) were effectively tied in win rate, while Ferrari actually secured more race victories (37 versus 35). This suggests that competitive balance was considerably higher than in the 2000s, 2010s, and 2020s.

A methodological caveat is that the dataset records some teams under multiple constructor names (for example, Lotus-Climax and Lotus-Ford in the 1960s). Their results were intentionally not merged because names such as "Lotus" and "Renault" may refer to distinct constructor entries across different historical periods.
